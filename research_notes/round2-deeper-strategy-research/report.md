# Round Two Demands Better Execution Decisions

**No—not all techniques reviewed in `RESEARCH.md` have been tried on Round 2.** Several were tested only on Round 1 or `sample_v2`, several entered Round 2 only inside multi-change strategies, and important latency, survival, inventory-value, and event-flow methods remain untested. The relevant baseline is now **`hmm_mm_v2`: 515,547 on the public tape**, versus **502,258 for `hmm_mm`**; the improvement is within the roughly **26,000 standard error**, so the two should be treated as statistically tied anchors rather than as proof that v2 is superior ([`hmm_mm_v2` docstring](../../strategies/round2/hmm_mm_v2.py)). The exact Bayesian grid, exact IOC-price oracles, passive signal-side entries, forced exits, current-edge size scaling, and the ID-gap jump detector have already failed on Round 2, while the per-second `RegimeFilter` has only a confounded combined test ([Round-2 research log](../../docs/RESEARCH.md)). The best remaining path is therefore not another hidden-price estimator or an invisible-IOC reconstruction: it is to value **what will still be executable 5 ms after an action**, to adapt the working scalar filter to observed permanent displacements, and to measure when queue survival and marginal inventory capacity actually matter. Older survival-analysis and mixed market/limit-order control methods fit those decisions well, while newer robust changepoint and heavy-tail state methods supply small engineering analogues—not ready-made trading rules or evidence of PnL. Every candidate below is a proposal unless a local result is explicitly reported; with one noisy public tape, small single-run gains cannot settle them.

## Round Two leaves a narrower but richer experiment space

Round 2 changes the economics of Round 1 rather than merely its optimal parameters. The locally fitted generator still exposes offset-corrected visible limit prices, but it adds permanent jumps in hidden value; ordinary passive fills consequently have negative markouts even though profitable quiet making and signal-driven crossing coexist inside `hmm_mm`. Original IOC request prices, nonexecuting IOCs, and their unfilled residual quantities are **not observable online**; executed sweep prices and quantities remain useful auxiliary evidence, not reconstructions of those hidden fields ([generator analysis](../../docs/DATA_ANALYSIS.md), [callback semantics](../../docs/MARKET.md)). The audit below treats “tested” as a Round-2 backtest, “combined/partial” as a related or confounded test, “earlier only” as evidence outside Round 2, and “untried” as no documented Round-2 experiment.

## Posterior execution value should lead the next experiments

The priorities below change one mathematical decision at a time on `hmm_mm_v2`, with `hmm_mm` as the clean secondary anchor. None predicts an exogenous jump before evidence arrives. The first five are genuinely distinct from completed failures; later ideas need explicit stop conditions so that broader modeling does not become another parameter-mining exercise.

**1. Measure posterior executable edge and 5 ms shadow survival first.** The current policy compares a point-estimate fair value with the current touch; it does not propagate uncertainty or test whether executable depth remains when the order becomes live ([`hmm_mm_v2`](../../strategies/round2/hmm_mm_v2.py), [latency rules](../../docs/MARKET.md)). For direction \(d\), proposed current-touch price \(p\), latency \(L=5\) ms, posterior value mean \(\mu_L\), and variance \(v_L=P+qL\), define \(z=[d(\mu_L-p)-c_0]/\sqrt{v_L}\). Keep the existing deterministic signal terms when constructing \(\mu_L\); freeze them for this first ablation. If \(s\) is the online shadow-estimated probability that visible depth still crosses \(p\) at maturity, the proposed statistics are **\(P_{\rm prof}=s\Phi(z)\)** and **\(EV_{\rm lot}=s[d(\mu_L-p)-c_0]\)**. Probability and expected advantage answer different questions, so both need predetermined thresholds; a positive-part expectation that discards adverse outcomes would be wrong. Gao and Wang’s latency model (2018, revised 2020) establishes latency as a real market-making risk, not this exchange-specific formula ([Gao–Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.05849)).

The exact smallest sequence is **B0 baseline; B1 posterior gate only with \(s=1\); B2 shadow survival only with the existing point-estimate edge; B3 joint gate**. Start at the best touch only, retaining existing sizes, inventory accounting, and order acknowledgement. Sample virtual buy-at-ask and sell-at-bid candidates on a fixed low-frequency schedule, such as every 50 ms, independently of whether the final gate would trade; use a fixed tiny table of side × regime × three signed-standardized-edge cells. Mature each at 5 ms and count visible survival, then audit delayed 1/5/30 s markouts and the dependence between survival and signed movement. Report cross count, lots, missed positive-value candidates, halves, regime PnL, standard error, and penalties. This differs from rejected probability-scaled edges by estimating the **actual action race**, and from deep sweeps by explicitly excluding them. Shadow survival does not guarantee full-quantity fills or eventual exit value, and the initial product \(s\Phi(z)\) is a conditional-independence approximation to audit rather than a theorem.

**2. Test a sparse process-mixture KF, without reviving the grid.** Aravkin, Burke and Pillonetto (2014) show that heavy-tailed process noise lets Student-t Kalman smoothers track sudden state changes, whereas heavy-tailed measurement noise addresses outliers; their solver is an offline MAP smoother, so the online scalar proposal here is an engineering analogue, not its proved algorithm ([Aravkin–Burke–Pillonetto](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.5588)). Before each ordinary offset-corrected visible-limit observation, use the existing prior \(N(m,P)\), reading \(y\), and noise \(R\). Compare ordinary innovation variance \(V_0=P+R\) with a sparse displaced-process hypothesis \(V_1=P+J+R\), Bayes-reweight them using a small prior mass \(\pi\), perform the two scalar KF updates, and collapse the posterior mean and variance. The collapsed variance **must include the between-hypothesis mean disagreement**. Offset and measurement-noise nuisance updates should use the posterior ordinary weight and clipped contributions so that one coherent move updates S rather than contaminating both nuisance estimates; do not reject a genuine large price reading merely because it is large.

Run filter diagnostics before strategy scoring: regime-specific S-error/corr² where available, standardized-innovation calibration, frequency and size of jump-posterior weight, and ordinary-period drift in offset/noise. The minimal sequence is **A1 process mixture only**, **A2 robust nuisance updates only**, then **A3 both**, all against the identical observations and policy. This differs from the grid by using constant memory and no dynamic price lattice, from the gap detector by requiring no IDs or fixed jump direction/size, and from deterministic IOC inflation by reacting to the visible-limit innovation itself. It remains **unproven, adds no observations, and is not equivalent to new information**. A bad \(\pi\) or \(J\) can overreact, freeze, or lag; mixture collapse can discard useful multimodality. It must not be packaged as jump prediction.

**3. Separate continuous volatility from permanent jump exposure.** Corsi, Pirino and Renò (2010) introduced threshold bipower variation to separate continuous quadratic variation from discontinuous components and study volatility forecasting; that result does not validate irregular tick-rounded S observations here or predict post-jump direction ([Corsi–Pirino–Renò](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/4435/)). The small local analogue is a fixed-clock estimator using decayed squared filtered-S increments and adjacent absolute-increment products for continuous variation, with a separate accumulator for observations independently classified as permanent displacement. Its purpose is to stop a 30–100 tick level shift from inflating one volatility scalar for minutes and suppressing subsequent ordinary crosses.

Begin with **D0: logging only**, comparing continuous and jump components around event windows and in quiet chunks. On `hmm_mm_v2`, **D1 should replace only the volatile-state diffusion scalar used in the KF with the continuous estimate**, retaining the current HMM posterior, fair-value formula, order prices, cap, and size; the jump accumulator remains a separate reported state. Only if D1 is calibrated should D2 change any edge/flow normalization, and only afterward should a separate adverse-gap exposure cap include inventory plus live resting and in-flight exposure. This one-change discipline avoids confusing estimation, risk caps, and jump following. Classical bipower asymptotics do not carry over exactly to sparse tick-rounded readings, and a handful of jumps cannot support a stable extreme quantile.

**4. Diagnose pre-sweep thin depth plus signed S lean.** Zheng, Moulines and Abergel (2012) found trade sign, market-order size, and best-quote liquidity informative for predicting their defined LOB price jump—a market order executing beyond the prior best—in French equities ([Zheng–Moulines–Abergel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.1381)). The transferable target is a **coming executable sweep**, not prediction of the generator’s exogenous S jumps. Original IOC size is unavailable here, so use only touch depth, depth removal, spread, recently executed volume, and the existing signed \(\hat S\)-touch gap. First measure out-of-sample log loss and event precision/recall by time chunk for a tiny binned score or logistic rule, with no strategy change. If a stable signal exists, the smallest backtest changes only the adversely exposed **opening** resting quote when the predetermined score fires: cancel/shrink that side while preserving passive unwinds, all fair values, existing crosses, and size. Accept that the cancel can lose the 5 ms race. This differs from the rejected post-sweep guard because the decision precedes the sweep, and from the failed gap detector because it uses continuous visible state rather than invisible-event identity. A negative diagnostic ends the idea; it does not justify trying many alarm thresholds until one wins.

**5. Price passive unwinds with fill-versus-invalidation competing risks.** Lo, MacKinlay and Zhang (2002) modeled actual limit-order execution times using survival analysis and warned that hypothetical first-passage or transaction-based fills can be poor proxies for real executions ([Lo–MacKinlay–Zhang](https://web.mit.edu/Alo/www/Papers/limit10.html)). Arroyo, Cartea, Moreno-Pino and Zohren (2023) extended state-dependent fill-time survival to LOBs with a convolutional Transformer; only the survival target transfers, not the deep estimator ([Arroyo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05479)). Locally, shadow quotes should record queue ahead and update it from trades and snapshot quantities, never from ghost cancel rows; small decayed hazard tables estimate fills and S-invalidations per lot at risk in bins of price distance, log queue ahead, signed S gap, spread, and independently validated jump state.

The smallest strategy change affects **only retention of an order that reduces existing inventory**: keep waiting when estimated fill-before-invalidation probability exceeds a predefined threshold; otherwise take the existing unwind action. Leave all passive entries, directional taking, target inventory, and quote prices unchanged. Report fill-survival calibration, time to flat, spread/depth paid, unwind markouts, and ordinary versus jump-window PnL. This is not the queue-front filter, which used a static queue threshold, and not faster liquidation, which forced time/edge schedules. Shadow fills must reproduce insertion latency, price-time priority, matches, and invalidation before their estimates are trusted; strategy-driven cancels are not automatically non-informative censoring.

**6. Isolate `RegimeFilter` before judging it.** First add the tool as a no-action shadow and score its per-second probability against later observable storm statistics in held-out chunks. Then make the sole policy change **replace the regime-selector probability with `RegimeFilter.P(storm)` at the existing `P_ON` threshold**, leaving the price KF’s diffusion inputs, far trigger, momentum guard, quotes, caps, crossing logic, and exits unchanged. Compare that with the existing per-order HMM on the same file and seeds. Do not port `storm_mm`’s other changes or infer failure from its approximately 172k combined result. The per-second tool may add complementary arrival/spread evidence, but it is slower than the per-order filter and its independence-tempered likelihoods can still be misspecified ([`RegimeFilter`](../../tools/regime_filter.py)).

**7. Consider marginal inventory opportunity cost only after a blocked-value diagnostic.** Existing evidence says full positions are usually worth keeping and current-edge capacity sizing lost; therefore the next candidate must not flatten early, shrink all entries, exceed ±100, or alter passive unwinds. Reuse the fixed shadow stream to estimate the positive excess value of later validated same-direction opportunities and join it with taken/missed/blocked diagnostics. If blocked opportunities do not have positive survival-adjusted forward value, **stop**. Otherwise, for signed remaining room \(r=100-dI\), test a simple shadow price \(\lambda_d(I)=g(r)U_d\), where \(g=0\) above a small reserve \(R_0\) and ramps linearly to one at the cap, and \(U_d\) is a decayed mean of positive later excess value. Apply it **only to same-direction opening crosses** as \(qEV_{\rm lot}-q\lambda_d(I)\). Compare against a fixed extra edge near the cap with the same average strength; otherwise there is no evidence that online continuation value adds anything. This is a heuristic one-step continuation-value approximation, not an optimal DP policy. Report forgone-opportunity value, holding time, regime PnL, and 30 s markouts. A negative result should close this line rather than trigger a large control build.

**8. Add carry/spread accounting before interpreting any of these gains.** On a no-action full-log run, decompose PnL into **directional carry**, accumulated as prior signed inventory times each mid change, and **spread/execution capture**, accumulated from each fill’s signed difference between contemporaneous mid and execution price, with explicit reconciliation to final markout and fees/penalties. Split both by quiet/volatile state, jump windows, opening versus unwinding fills, and holding-time buckets. This is a diagnostic, not alpha: a strategy that wins only because inventory happened to face the jump direction has not demonstrated a better entry or queue rule. The same accounting should decide whether the inventory experiment is worth running and whether shadow-execution gains survive after removing directional luck ([original proposal in the research log](../../docs/RESEARCH.md)).

## Better validation infrastructure reduces mistakes rather than adding alpha

A proper Round-2 generator remains valuable because it would answer the unresolved question **what is perfect present-S knowledge worth**, and because matched seeds can separate mechanisms from one tape’s fortunate jumps. The first twin attempt was dropped for a real reason: one 6,000 s public realization did not consistently identify regime durations, offsets, diffusion, and jumps. A later path-based twin improved the price-path calibration, but its flow-to-price and inventory responses remained wrong: several quiet-regime tuning gains on synthetic seeds reversed on the real public tape. Restart only with a staged acceptance test: jointly fit switch rates, regime-specific offset distributions, diffusion, jump size/rate, and IOC impact using the forward model; compare held-out spread, offset, sweep, impact, regime-duration, jump-spacing, order-flow response, and strategy-relevant markout distributions; and stop if the generator cannot reproduce them. The joint MLE is **offline research infrastructure**, not a recommendation to rerun the failed grid. After validation, the smallest oracle replaces only v2’s \(\hat S\) with true S on matched seeds, leaving every policy rule unchanged, and compares true-S, filtered-S, and current/future-mid oracles without mistaking future events for present-state knowledge ([unfinished Round-2 program](../../docs/RESEARCH.md)).

Once credible tapes exist, use **identical matched seeds for baseline and candidate**, include calibrated jump/no-jump and mirrored-jump-sign versions, select on training seeds, and report held-out seeds separately. Rank candidates by **mean PnL − 0.25 × cross-seed standard deviation**, a previously proposed practitioner risk-adjusted selection heuristic rather than a theoretically optimal scoring rule, and keep drawdown, breach penalty, inventory quantiles, event-window PnL, and per-lot markouts beside it ([research log](../../docs/RESEARCH.md)). Stress continuous volatility, IOC impact, jump rate/size, spread, and cancel rates independently; mirroring is particularly important because long/short inventory exposure can make a one-sided public drift look like skill. These tools improve evidence quality, **not direct alpha**. Until the Round-2 generator is accepted, an existing separate validation tape can test generalization but cannot support claims about matched true-S performance.

Finally, audit the exchange-facing state machine independently of PnL. Log sends/cancels/modifies that mature at 5 ms, private acknowledgement latency, partial fills, cancel-race fills, rejected shrinks, duplicate impulses, actual executable sweep slippage, and worst-case inventory including live resting exposure. Every `NORMAL`, pending, and follow state needs a valid route back to normal; measure time stuck unable to act. A candidate with better nominal PnL but unaccounted orders, frequent state lockup, or penalties is not a valid improvement. These checks are especially important for shadow survival because visible depth at maturity is not proof of full-size execution.

## The next iteration should avoid already closed paths

| Do not spend the next iteration on | Decisive reason |
|---|---|
| **Invisible IOC reconstruction or any two-channel filter requiring original IOC request prices** | They are unobservable; gaps do not identify event side/price/size, and even exact-price oracles lost. Executed sweep price/volume are auxiliary bounds only ([research log](../../docs/RESEARCH.md)). |
| **ID-gap/clamp fixed-jump detector** | Direct backtest loss, 192 alarms for 22 large moves, and unresolved online identity. Detection from other evidence remains possible; this channel is closed. |
| **Another full Bayesian grid, generic particle filter, or large latent-state simulator** | The exact grid lost badly and had the wrong volatile posterior. More approximation compute adds no information; only the explicitly labelled small process-mixture experiment remains distinct. |
| **Forced exits, early flattening, stronger unwind skew, deliberate inventory overshoot, or current-edge size scaling** | Broad direct Round-2 evidence is negative and full positions retained value. A near-cap continuation-value experiment must not smuggle these mechanisms back in. |
| **Passive signal-side entries, penny-jumping, deep calm sweeps, or another reactive post-sweep guard** | These specific mechanisms have already lost; profitable quiet making is not a reason to reopen them. |
| **Blanket one-tick censoring, extra mean IOC-push terms, and more fitted OLS fair values** | Each has direct adverse or neutral evidence; the same observation likelihood is already present in the working/filtered approaches. |
| **Fixed execution schedules and transient-resilience splitting** | There is no parent quantity/deadline, and the local fitted IOC impact is permanent. Waiting for transient recovery attacks the wrong cost model ([generator fit](../../docs/DATA_ANALYSIS.md)). |
| **Full finite-difference HJB/HJBQVI, deep survival nets, deep LOB models, or RL** | They add heavy state fitting or unavailable learned weights without addressing the measured bottleneck. Keep their discrete action and survival targets, not their solvers/networks ([Law–Viens](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.07222), [Arroyo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05479)). |
| **Default Hawkes/ACD guards, EVT-only jump timers, or persistent fixed post-jump storm de-risking** | Raw arrivals are fitted as Poisson, permanent level shifts are not transient storms, and no stable pre-jump timing signal has been established. Require residual-memory evidence and separate continuous from jump risk. |
| **Contest-specific exploits** | Cross-instrument hedges, recurring bots, participant-ID folklore, T+1 rules, message-cap tricks, seed recovery, and unverified contest rankings do not fit this one-instrument fixed tape. |

## A gated experiment order prevents another public-tape sweep

The concrete order below follows **diagnostic → one-change backtest → matched multi-seed validation** wherever the required generator exists. A failed diagnostic closes its branch rather than promoting a broader model.

| Order | Diagnostic and acceptance gate | Single-change public-tape experiment | Validation and reporting |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Re-run only the immutable baseline accounting on existing logs: PnL, quiet/volatile split, carry/capture, inventory, penalties, and state/race audit. | No strategy change. | Establish the exact comparison artifact and reconciliation before testing candidates. |
| **1** | Collect fixed-schedule 5 ms shadow candidates; verify insertion/maturity semantics, fill calibration by cell, and delayed markouts. | **B1 posterior only**, then **B2 survival only**, then **B3 joint**; never bundle A. | On matched seeds, compare crosses, lots, missed value, PnL mean−0.25SD, drawdown, and markouts. |
| **2** | Check process-mixture innovation calibration and nuisance stability; reject settings that obscure ordinary-period drift. | **A1**, **A2**, **A3** independently of B. If both families win separately, test one prespecified interaction only afterward. | Matched jump/no-jump seeds plus mirrored signs; report false adaptation and event-window delay/PnL. |
| **3** | Validate continuous-versus-jump variance separation around known events. | **D1 KF diffusion only**; defer edge and exposure changes. | Confirm post-jump traded value is not suppressed while quiet behavior and penalties remain stable. |
| **4** | Require held-out pre-sweep predictive value after current features. | One predetermined thin-depth/S guard on opening quotes only. | Event precision/recall, cancel-race fills, avoided adverse fills, and lost profitable quiet fills across seeds. |
| **5** | Validate shadow queue reconstruction and competing-risk fill/invalidation calibration. | Passive-unwind retention only. | Compare time-to-flat, spread paid, unwind markouts, and PnL independently of directional carry. |
| **6** | Shadow-score the per-second `RegimeFilter` in held-out chunks. | Replace only the policy regime selector; no `storm_mm` machinery. | Test on separate tapes/seeds and report confusion plus PnL by actual event window. |
| **7** | Estimate positive value of opportunities actually blocked near the cap; stop if it is absent. | Marginal same-direction entry shadow price versus a matched static near-cap edge. | Holding time, forgone value, inventory quantiles, 30 s markouts, and multi-seed risk-adjusted score. |
| **8** | Only after earlier evidence justifies them, evaluate queue survival tables, thin-queue/inventory interaction, and queue engineering one at a time. | One frozen rule or table per candidate; no DP bundle. | Training/validation seed separation and explicit comparison with the simpler rejected static rule. |
| **9** | Fit residual intensity memory for raw and executed streams; accept only reproducible non-Poisson residuals. | If gated in, replace only the arrival-count flow component with the smallest marked intensity. | Time-rescaled residual QQ/autocorrelation, held-out log score, and PnL; otherwise close Hawkes/ACD. |
| **Research track** | Stage-gate the Round-2 joint MLE/generator; do not tune strategies while fitting it. | Only after acceptance, true-S replacement with unchanged v2 policy. | Use matched unseen seeds for all finalists; if fit fails again, do not substitute future-mid oracles for true S. |

Public Round 2 is one 6,000-second realization dominated by a small number of jumps. Its roughly **23k–26k standard-error scale**, heavy event dependence, and many already tried variants mean that a few-thousand gain is noise and even a 13k baseline difference is not decisive ([research log](../../docs/RESEARCH.md), [`hmm_mm_v2`](../../strategies/round2/hmm_mm_v2.py)). Pre-register the single mechanism, parameters, and failure metric; inspect halves and event windows; use common random numbers and unseen seeds when available; and reserve the public tape for confirmation rather than iterative optimization. If no validated Round-2 generator emerges, report the uncertainty honestly instead of treating reshuffles or repeated public runs as independent evidence.

## Conclusion

The audit changes the research objective from recovering more hidden information to **making better decisions under observable execution risk**. The strongest closed results eliminate seductive detours: exact hidden IOC prices do not pay, an exact price-state lattice is not inherently superior, and freeing inventory by force destroys valuable positions. The open mechanisms are consequently smaller and more testable—arrival-time value, queue survival, robust adaptation after visible displacement, and carefully measured continuation value.

The right unit of progress is no longer the best number on the public tape. It is a calibrated diagnostic followed by a one-rule causal ablation that survives matched sessions, mirrored jump directions, and race/inventory audits. That discipline gives even a negative result decision value, while another lucky public-tape improvement would leave the same unresolved uncertainty.
